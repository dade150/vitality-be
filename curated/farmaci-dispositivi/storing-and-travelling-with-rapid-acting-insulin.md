## 1. Storing and Travelling with Rapid-Acting Insulin: Scope

This guide summarizes general storage and travel advice from the NHS for rapid-acting insulin. It is relevant to people with type 2 diabetes who use rapid-acting insulin as part of their prescribed treatment.

The source lists the following brand names: Admelog, Apidra, Fiasp, Humalog, Lyumjev, NovoRapid, and Trurapi.

Storage conditions and in-use expiry periods can vary by insulin product and presentation. Always check the patient information leaflet for the exact insulin being used.

## 2. Storing Unopened Insulin at Home

- Keep unopened disposable insulin pens, cartridges, or vials in a refrigerator, following the product's storage instructions.
- Keep insulin away from the freezer compartment and cooling elements.
- Insulin that has frozen must not be used. Follow the product instructions and ask a pharmacist how to dispose of it safely.
- Plan supplies with the diabetes care team and keep an appropriate reserve available, as advised for the individual treatment plan.

The required storage temperature and other conditions should be checked in the leaflet for the specific product.

## 3. Storing Insulin in Use

According to the NHS source, a new disposable pen or cartridge of the rapid-acting insulin products covered may be kept at room temperature for up to 4 weeks once in use.

Important precautions:

- Protect insulin from direct sunlight and heat sources such as radiators.
- Do not allow insulin to freeze.
- Follow the product-specific instructions for room-temperature limits and the maximum period of use after opening or first use.
- Record the date the insulin was first taken out of the refrigerator or started in use, as appropriate for the product, to help track its expiry period.
- Do not rely on insulin that has exceeded its permitted in-use period. Ask a pharmacist about replacement and safe disposal.

Cold insulin may be more uncomfortable to inject. Follow the product instructions before allowing refrigerated insulin to reach room temperature.

**Important:** The 4-week period is not universal for all insulin products. Check the leaflet for the exact brand, formulation, and pen or cartridge.

## 4. Preparing Insulin Supplies for Travel

Before travelling:

- Take an adequate supply of insulin and the necessary injection equipment, including spare supplies where appropriate.
- Ask the diabetes care team how much extra insulin to carry.
- Find out how insulin can be obtained at the destination in case supplies are lost or run out.
- Carry a recent prescription or suitable medical documentation when useful for obtaining medicines or explaining the need to carry diabetes equipment.
- Check the storage requirements for the insulin and plan how to protect it from heat and freezing.

A cool bag may be useful when necessary. Keep insulin separated from ice packs or frozen cooling elements so that it does not freeze.

## 5. Flying with Insulin

The NHS source recommends carrying insulin in hand luggage when flying.

Travellers may also need:

- A letter from a GP or diabetes care team confirming the need to carry insulin and related equipment.
- Information about the insulin, pens, needles, pump, or glucose-monitoring devices being carried.
- Advance contact with the airline if using an insulin pump or wearable glucose monitor.

Check current airline, airport-security, and destination requirements before departure. Device-specific guidance may apply to airport scanners or other screening equipment.

## 6. Crossing Time Zones

Travel across several time zones can affect insulin timing and diabetes management.

Before departure, ask the diabetes care team for an individualized plan covering insulin timing and doses. Do not independently change the insulin dose or schedule based on a general travel guide.

## 7. If Insulin May Have Been Stored Incorrectly

If insulin has frozen, been exposed to excessive heat, or remained outside its permitted storage conditions for too long:

- Check the product leaflet for instructions.
- Ask a pharmacist or diabetes care professional whether the insulin remains suitable for use.
- Arrange replacement if advised.
- Follow the recommended plan for monitoring glucose and maintaining access to prescribed treatment.

Do not assume insulin is safe or effective simply because its appearance has not changed.

## 8. Important Limitations

This guide provides general information about rapid-acting insulin and travel. It does not replace the product-specific leaflet, airline requirements, or individualized advice from a diabetes care team.

Storage temperatures, expiry periods after opening, and transport requirements vary between products and jurisdictions. The source reflects UK guidance; local arrangements in Italy may differ.
